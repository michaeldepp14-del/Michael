## 2. Standard homeowners (HO-3) policies: what pays and what does not

### Takeaway
Standard Florida homeowners and renters policies exclude rising-water flood damage; only a separate flood policy pays. Local reporting on this event confirms families are finding their homeowners claims do not cover it.

### Cited Findings
- Standard homeowners and renters policies exclude rising-water flood damage; only a flood policy pays — [cfldr.com](https://www.cfldr.com/florida-flooding); [WFTV "Homeowners insurance may not help with Central Florida flooding"](https://www.wftv.com/news/local/homeowners-insurance-may-not-help-with-central-florida-flooding/E6R6NI2OXVBELCYAQLPX5J3VQE/)
- Spectrum News 13 (Nov 11, 2026): flood coverage is "the largest insurance gap experts see" — [Spectrum News 13](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2026/11/11/flood-coverage-is-the-largest-insurance-gap-experts-see)
- Sewer backup: under an NFIP policy, sewer backup is covered only if it is a direct result of flooding; otherwise not — [FloodSmart "What flood insurance covers"](https://www.floodsmart.gov/how/what-is-covered)
- In Florida OIR data for Hurricane Isaias (Oct 9, 2026 landfall near Destin; data as of Nov 6, 2026), 326 homeowners claims were closed without payment specifically because the damage was flood and the owner had no flood policy; OIR lists flood damage outside homeowners coverage as a common no-pay reason — [Insurance Journal, 2026-11-10](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2026/11/10/892259.htm)

### Inferences
- A Brevard homeowner with only an HO-3 and rising-water damage from Nov 1–2 should expect a denial on the structure and contents; the homeowners policy remains relevant only for non-flood causes (for example, roof leak from wind damage).

### Gaps
- Not verified this session (background knowledge only, no source opened): HO-3 covers rain entering through a wind-created opening; a water/sewer-backup endorsement (commonly $5,000–$25,000 limits) covers drain backup not caused by surface flood. Needs a citation before use.
- No data on how many Brevard homeowners filed HO-3 claims for this event or the denial rate.

## 3. NFIP: limits, exclusions, deadlines, payouts, premiums, authorization

### Takeaway
NFIP pays up to $250,000 building and $100,000 contents on a home, with no living-expense coverage; proof of loss is due in 60 days unless FEMA extends it, and no extension for this event was found. The program is authorized only until December 11, 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Limits and coverage**
- Residential limits: building up to $250,000, contents up to $100,000, bought separately with separate deductibles; limits are statutory — [FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/how/what-is-covered); [FEMA Summary of Coverage brochure (12/2023)](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/fema-nfip-summary-coverage-brochure-12-2023.pdf)
- Not covered: living and financial costs (temporary housing / additional living expenses), outdoor property, some valuables, most vehicles, damage that could have been avoided — [FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/how/what-is-covered)
- Basements: limited coverage; most contents and finishes below the lowest elevated floor are not covered — [FEMA Summary of Coverage brochure](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/fema-nfip-summary-coverage-brochure-12-2023.pdf)
- Increased Cost of Compliance (ICC): up to $30,000 to elevate, floodproof, relocate, or demolish to meet local floodplain rules; requires building coverage; no deductible — [CoveragePrep NFIP guide](https://coverageprep.com/guides/nfip-flood-insurance-claims/); [NFIP adjuster claims manual, ICC section](https://www.lavislaw.com/files/nfip_adjuster_claims_manual_part3.pdf)
- Waiting period: new NFIP policies take 30 days to become effective (exception when tied to a home-purchase closing); private policies also have waiting periods — [Spectrum News 13, 2026-11-19](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2026/11/19/homeowners-can-consider-national-and-private-flood-insurance-policies)

**Claim deadlines and speed**
- Signed, sworn proof of loss due within 60 days of the loss, even if the adjuster does not supply the form — [United Policyholders](https://uphelp.org/claim-guidance-publications/flood-insurance-claim-deadlines/?print=print); [The Claim Organizer](https://theclaimorganizer.com/resources/nfip-flood-insurance-proof-of-loss)
- FEMA has extended the window after major events (60 → 120 days after Hurricane Milton, 2024) — [The Claim Organizer](https://theclaimorganizer.com/resources/nfip-flood-insurance-proof-of-loss)
- Advance payments, when FEMA authorizes them: up to $5,000 pre-inspection, up to $20,000 with photos and verified expenses; deducted from the final payment — [CoveragePrep](https://coverageprep.com/guides/nfip-flood-insurance-claims/)
- Payment due 60 days after the insurer receives proof of loss, once amount is agreed — [CoveragePrep](https://coverageprep.com/guides/nfip-flood-insurance-claims/)
- Policyholders do not need to wait for a disaster declaration to file — [cfldr.com](https://www.cfldr.com/florida-flooding)

**Payout levels (Florida, recent events)**
- NFIP paid $7.5 billion on 76,000+ claims for Helene and Milton combined: $6.3 billion on 54,622 Helene claims, $1.2 billion on 21,468 Milton claims — [FEMA fact sheet, Florida Helene and Milton Recovery](https://www.fema.gov/fact-sheet/florida-helene-and-milton-recovery-0)
- Third-party tabulation of FEMA data: average paid NFIP claim from Helene in Florida $130,656; about one-third of Helene claims closed without payment; Florida 10-year (2017–2026) average paid claim $101,497 — [FloodHawk Florida](https://floodhawk.com/florida/)

**Premiums in Brevard (Risk Rating 2.0)**
- 42,355 NFIP policies in force in Brevard County as of Aug 31, 2026; average annual cost $736 including federal policy fee (24% below Florida average of $965); average coverage $288,345 building + contents — [search summary attributing to a Brevard flood-insurance page](https://brevardcoastalhomes.com/flood-insurance) (attribution among results uncertain; candidate alternative: [floodzonemap.org Brevard](https://floodzonemap.org/florida/brevard-county))
- Alternative count: 40,923 policies written/renewed over a trailing 12 months, average premium $696, $28.5M total annual premium — [fludzone.com](https://www.fludzone.com/flood-zones/florida/palm-bay)
- June 2026 transactions: 4,648 new/renewed Brevard NFIP policies averaged $718 premium, $933 total policy cost — [Out of the Storm News](https://outofthestormnews.com/flood-insurance/costs/florida/brevard-county)
- Older table: Brevard current cost $668 vs full risk-based cost $2,367 (policy count 21,211; date unclear) — [Clovered](https://clovered.com/flood-insurance/florida/)
- Reporter estimate for a $400,000 home in parts of Brevard: $900–$1,800/yr NFIP, $850–$1,500/yr private — [Spectrum News 13, 2026-11-19](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2026/11/19/homeowners-can-consider-national-and-private-flood-insurance-policies)
- Annual premium increases capped at 18% per year for most policies — [FEMA Florida Risk Rating 2.0 state profile, 04/2025](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_florida-state-profile_04-2025.pdf)
- Palm Bay's CRS rating gives SFHA policyholders up to a 15% discount — [fludzone.com Palm Bay](https://www.fludzone.com/flood-zones/florida/palm-bay)

**Authorization status**
- NFIP is authorized through December 11, 2026 (CRS Insight updated Sept 11, 2026; NAR FAQ). Most recent lapse: Oct 1 – Nov 12, 2025, retroactively reauthorized. During a lapse NFIP cannot issue new or renewal policies; existing claims continue to be paid — [CRS IN10835](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN10835.html); [NAR FAQ](https://www.nar.realtor/flood-insurance/faq-national-flood-insurance-program-expires-december-11-2026)

### Inferences
- As of Nov 25, 2026 the NFIP is in force, but with a 30-day wait and a Dec 11 expiry, a Brevard homeowner buying a policy today may hit the lapse window before coverage starts if Congress does not act.
- Default proof-of-loss deadline for a Nov 2, 2026 loss is roughly Jan 1, 2027, absent an extension.
- Brevard's $668–$736 average versus a $2,367 full-risk figure suggests most policies are still on the 18% glide path upward.

### Gaps
- No FEMA bulletin found extending the proof-of-loss deadline or authorizing advance payments for the Nov 2026 Florida flooding. Unknown, not "none."
- No NFIP claim count or paid total for this event located (OpenFEMA unreachable).
- No search result after early October 2026 on congressional action on the Dec 11 expiry.
- Not verified this session (background knowledge): non-residential/commercial limits of $500,000 building and $500,000 contents; standard deductible options roughly $1,000–$10,000 per coverage; FEMA appeal window of 60 days from a written denial and one-year suit deadline in federal court.
- Brevard premium sources are mostly commercial sites republishing FEMA data; no official FEMA county figure was opened.

## 4. Flood insurance take-up: who is actually insured

### Takeaway
Roughly 12–17% of Brevard homes carry NFIP coverage (about 41,000–42,000 policies), so the large majority of flooded homes were probably uninsured for flood. No city-level counts or SFHA split for this event were found.

### Cited Findings
- About 11.8% of residential properties in Brevard County are covered by NFIP, attributed to FEMA — [Spectrum News 13, 2026-11-11](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2026/11/11/flood-coverage-is-the-largest-insurance-gap-experts-see); same 11.8% on an agency page ~7 months old — [Anchor Atlas Insurance](https://anchoratlasinsurance.com/florida-insurance/brevard)
- Conflicting figure: about 1 in 6 Brevard households (17%) carries NFIP coverage — [fludzone.com](https://www.fludzone.com/flood-zones/florida/palm-bay); also repeated in [cfldr.com](https://www.cfldr.com/florida-flooding)
- Brevard NFIP policies in force: 42,355 (Aug 31, 2026) or 40,923 (trailing 12 months) — see section 3 sources
- Florida had 1,767,045 NFIP policies in force as of April 2026, 38.93% of the national total — [NAIC private flood slides, May 2026](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/call_materials/PrivateFlood_May2026_0.pdf)
- Private flood, national: about 683,000 policies in force at end of 2025 (possibly ~100,000 higher due to a reporting error); GAO puts private at 14% of all flood policies in 2025 — [NAIC, May 2026](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/call_materials/PrivateFlood_May2026_0.pdf); [GAO-27-108012](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-27-108012.pdf)
- Private flood, Florida: only a dated figure found, 3% of Florida flood policies in 2018 — [Wharton issue brief](https://esg.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Florida-Private-Flood-Issue-Brief.pdf)
- Homes outside FEMA flood zones flooded in this event; example Palm Bay resident with no flood insurance, flooded by an overflowing drainage canal, living in a camper in her driveway — [Spectrum News 13, 2026-11-19](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2026/11/19/homeowners-can-consider-national-and-private-flood-insurance-policies)

- Because inland Palm Bay and West Melbourne are largely Zone X and the flooding was rainfall/canal driven, take-up among flooded homes was plausibly below the county average, which is weighted toward barrier-island and riverfront SFHA policies. This is an inference, not a measured figure.

## 5. Citizens Property Insurance flood requirement

### Takeaway
Citizens policyholders with dwelling coverage of $400,000 or more have had to carry flood insurance since Jan 1, 2026; every remaining Citizens personal-lines policy (except condo units) must have it from Jan 1, 2027, regardless of flood zone.

### Cited Findings
- Phase-in by Coverage A dwelling value: $600,000+ effective Jan 1, 2024; $400,000+ effective Jan 1, 2026; all policies regardless of value effective Jan 1, 2027 — [Citizens flood page](https://www.citizensfla.com/flood)
- Applies regardless of FEMA flood zone; condominium unit-owner policies are exempt — [Citizens news release](https://www.citizensfla.com/-/new-flood-rules-could-save-you-money); [News4Jax, 2025-09-16](https://news4jax.com/news/local/2025/09/16/flood-insurance-will-be-mandatory-for-most-citizens-property-insurance-policyholders-by-2027)
- The test is replacement cost (Coverage A), not market or assessed value; Citizens accepts the NFIP maximum ($250,000) where more is unavailable — [Citizens flood page](https://www.citizensfla.com/flood)
- Hillsborough County notice (Nov 13, 2026) confirming the Jan 1, 2027 final phase — [hcfl.gov](https://hcfl.gov/newsroom/2026/11/13/flood-coverage-becomes-mandatory-for-citizens-property-insurance-customers-in-2027)
- Enacting law: SB 2-A (2022), ch. 2022-271 — [Florida Senate bill summary](https://www.flsenate.gov/Committees/BillSummaries/2022A/html/2)

### Inferences
- At the time of the Nov 2026 flood, Brevard Citizens customers with dwellings under $400,000 were not yet required to hold flood coverage; they must buy it to renew in 2027, after the loss.

### Gaps
- The intermediate step ($500,000+ effective Jan 1, 2025) and the 2023 start for wind-only policies in SFHAs are from background knowledge; the search summary did not show them.
- Number of Citizens policies in Brevard County and how many fall under each threshold: not found.

## 7. Flooded vehicles

### Takeaway
Flooded cars are covered only under the optional comprehensive part of an auto policy; liability-only drivers get nothing, and flood policies exclude vehicles.

### Cited Findings
- Comprehensive coverage pays for flood damage; liability-only does not — [The Zebra](https://www.thezebra.com/auto-insurance/coverage/natural-disasters/does-car-insurance-cover-flood-damage/)
- NFIP excludes most vehicles — [FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/how/what-is-covered)
- Vehicles submerged for extended periods are typically totaled; repaired cars may carry a salvage title — [Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/flood-car-faqs)
- Homes and cars flooded in Palm Bay (date of article not confirmed in results) — [WFTV](https://www.wftv.com/news/local/homeowners-insurance-may-not-help-with-central-florida-flooding/E6R6NI2OXVBELCYAQLPX5J3VQE/)

## 8. Florida claim-handling rules (apply to homeowners and private flood policies, not NFIP)

### Takeaway
Florida insurers must acknowledge a claim in 7 days and pay or deny in 60; policyholders have 1 year to give notice. Assignment of benefits and one-way attorney fees are gone for policies issued since 2023. DFS mediation is not available for NFIP claims.

### Cited Findings
- Acknowledge claim within 7 days of submittal (was 14 days; changed effective March 1, 2023) — [DFS Homeowner Claims Bill of Rights](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/docs-sf/consumer-services-libraries/consumerservices-documents/understanding-coverage/consumer-guides/english---homeowner-claims-bill-of-rights.pdf); [Fla. Stat. 627.70131 (2026)](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/627.70131)
- Pay or deny within 60 days for initial, reopened, or supplemental claims, unless prevented by factors beyond the insurer's control; adjuster's detailed estimate must be sent within 7 days of being generated; interest may be owed on late payment — [Fla. Stat. 627.70131 (2026)](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/627.70131)
- Notice of claim or reopened claim barred after 1 year from date of loss; supplemental claim barred after 18 months — [Fla. Stat. 627.70132](https://flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2025/627.70132); [JD Supra case note](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/florida-appellate-court-holds-that-5086967)
- Assignment of post-loss benefits prohibited for residential and commercial property policies issued on or after Jan 1, 2023 — [Florida Senate SB 2-A summary](https://www.flsenate.gov/Committees/BillSummaries/2022A/html/2)
- One-way attorney fees eliminated for property insurance suits; offer-of-judgment statute applies instead (SB 2-A, ch. 2022-271, effective Dec 16, 2022) — [Florida Senate SB 2-A summary](https://www.flsenate.gov/Committees/BillSummaries/2022A/html/2); [Clausen Miller](https://www.clausen.com/florida-eliminates-one-way-attorneys-fees-statute-in-bid-to-stabilize-insurance-market/)
- Public adjuster fees: capped at 10% for claims tied to a Governor-declared emergency for one year after the declaration, 20% otherwise (single secondary source; statute text not seen) — [DCS public adjusters compliance page](https://www.dcspia.com/florida-insurance-claim-laws/)
- DFS mediation: non-binding, insurer pays the mediator, conference within 21 days of assignment; NFIP claims are ineligible; non-NFIP (private) flood policies may be eligible — [DFS Request Mediation page](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/consumers/RequestMediation); [Fla. Admin. Code R. 69J-166.031](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-69J-166-031)

### Inferences
- EO 26-227 (Nov 2, 2026) should trigger the 10% public adjuster cap for claims from this event through about Nov 2, 2027, if the secondary source describes the statute correctly.
- NFIP claims follow federal rules (60-day proof of loss, FEMA appeal, federal court), so Florida's 7/60-day deadlines, mediation, and 1-year notice rule protect only homeowners-policy and private-flood claimants.

### Gaps
- Public adjuster cap not confirmed against Fla. Stat. 626.854 text.
- Whether OIR or DFS issued any emergency order for this event (claim-deadline tolling, cancellation moratorium, insurance villages): not found.

## 9. What uninsured homeowners get instead

### Takeaway
With DR-4956 approved, uninsured Brevard homeowners can get FEMA grants capped at $46,400 for housing and $46,400 for other needs, plus SBA loans; FEMA aid for a home in a Special Flood Hazard Area comes with a permanent obligation to carry flood insurance.

### Cited Findings
- FEMA Individuals and Households Program maximum for disasters declared on or after Oct 1, 2026: $46,400 housing assistance and $46,400 other needs assistance (91 FR 70094, published Nov 6, 2026). Prior year: $44,800 each — [Federal Register notice 2026-22693](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/06/2026-22693/notice-of-maximum-amount-of-assistance-under-the-individuals-and-households-program); [FY2026 notice 2026-19854](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/29/2026-19854/notice-of-maximum-amount-of-assistance-under-the-individuals-and-households-program)
- Aid under DR-4956 includes grants for temporary housing and home repairs and low-cost loans for uninsured property losses; apply at DisasterAssistance.gov, FEMA app, or 800-621-3362; file insurance claims first — [ClickOrlando, 2026-11-25](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/local/2026/11/25/heres-how-florida-storm-and-flood-victims-apply-for-fema-disaster-assistance/); [FOX 35](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/federal-disaster-aid-approved-florida-flood-recovery-continues-whos-eligible)

- Flood insurance condition: recipients of FEMA aid for flood-damaged property in an SFHA must obtain and maintain flood insurance for as long as they own the building; requirement passes to buyers; non-compliance bars future federal disaster aid — [FEMA, "Meeting FEMA's Federal Flood Insurance Requirement," 2025-08-21](https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20250821/meeting-femas-federal-flood-insurance-requirement)
- FEMA may buy a Group Flood Insurance Policy out of the grant: about $2,400 for a three-year term; owner must self-fund coverage afterward — [FEMA, same release](https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20250821/meeting-femas-federal-flood-insurance-requirement); [FEMA brochure 07/2024](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/fema-nfip-federal-disaster-assistance-meeting-the-flood-insurance-requirement-brochure-07-2024.pdf)
- Having flood insurance does not disqualify a household from FEMA help — [Quartz comparison](https://qz.com/flood-insurance-vs-fema-disaster-aid)

### Inferences
- The $46,400 cap applies because DR-4956 was declared Nov 24, 2026, after the Oct 1, 2026 threshold.
- The gap between an insured and uninsured outcome is large: NFIP ceiling $350,000 (building + contents) and a Florida average paid claim near $100,000–$130,000, versus a $46,400 housing-repair ceiling that few FEMA recipients reach.
- The insure-forward rule binds only SFHA properties; many flooded Zone X homes in Palm Bay/West Melbourne would receive aid without the mandate.
