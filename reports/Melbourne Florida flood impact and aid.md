# FEMA opens narrow aid for flooded Brevard

**Source caveat, stated once: every figure in this report comes from web-search result summaries retrieved on November 25, 2026. Page fetches were blocked, so no article, FEMA page, statute or county release was read in full. Treat every number as "reported in a search summary" and check it against the linked page before you publish or quote it. Conflicting figures are flagged where they occur.**

Nobody has a final count of flooded houses: Brevard County's categorized self-reports stood at **869 on November 23 and 952 on November 24**, the governor's November 9 letter says **more than 2,000 residents** reported home flooding, and the county's own spokesman says "thousands." The damage sits mostly in south Brevard (Palm Bay, West Melbourne, Malabar, Grant-Valkaria, Barefoot Bay) rather than Melbourne proper, with reported in-home depths of **1.5 to 5 feet**, and a second round on November 17-20 put the St. Johns River above its 1953 record and left hundreds of homes near Lake Poinsett under water. Most of those owners have no flood coverage: only **about 12-17% of Brevard homes carry an NFIP policy**, and standard homeowners policies exclude flood. The President approved a major disaster declaration, **FEMA DR-4956, on November 24, 2026**, which opens FEMA grants capped at **$46,400 for housing plus $46,400 for other needs**, a gap of roughly sevenfold against the **$350,000** an NFIP policy can pay. FEMA is not fixing the drainage: Public Assistance covers only debris removal and emergency measures, and mitigation money for buyouts and elevations is "under review." No FEMA application deadline, no dollars approved and no Brevard recovery center had been published as of November 25. For electricians and plumbers the demand is real and rule-driven, but funded work will arrive in waves from December 2026 onward as FEMA, NFIP and local SHIP money pays out.

## Two floods in three weeks, with 869 to 2,000-plus homes reported

The event has no storm name. The state calls it the "2026 Fall Flooding"; the governor's order attributes it to a broad area of low pressure ([WFTV](https://www.wftv.com/news/local/desantis-declares-state-emergency-7-florida-counties-after-severe-flooding/PXEG7FBYZZCWDKNC4PUQMTZ7P4/)). Round one fell on **November 1-2, 2026**, and NWS Melbourne issued a Flash Flood Emergency for West Melbourne and Palm Bay at 7:56 a.m. on November 2 ([The Watchers](https://watchers.news/2026/11/03/florida-declares-emergency-after-catastrophic-flooding-in-southern-brevard-county/)). Rainfall totals conflict by gauge and cutoff time: Malabar is reported at **24.10 inches** in one summary and **24.52 inches** in another, and Palm Bay at 19.75, 20.60 or 22 inches ([The Watchers](https://watchers.news/2026/11/03/florida-declares-emergency-after-catastrophic-flooding-in-southern-brevard-county/); [FOX 35](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/nearly-25-inches-rain-reported-brevard-county-historic-rainfall-floods-central-florida.amp); [cfldr.com](https://www.cfldr.com/florida-flooding)). The governor's request letter cites radar estimates of 25-30 inches in spots, and one outlet's "up to 40 inches" is an outlier that nothing else supports ([Governor's letter](https://haridopolos.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/haridopolos.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/expedited-major-disaster-declaration-request-2026-fall-flooding-compressed.pdf)). Parts of Palm Bay, Malabar and Grant-Valkaria exceeded the 1-in-1,000-year rainfall threshold ([WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/2026-11-03/floridas-dry-season-opens-under-water)). The City of Melbourne's 11.5 inches on November 2 is described as its wettest day on record, but that comes from an aggregator and is unverified ([Nature World News](https://www.natureworldnews.com/articles/73940/20261119/stalled-storm-pushes-st-johns-river-past-its-1953-record-level-3-flood-threat-shifts-floridas.htm)).

Round two ran about **November 17-20**. Brevard had new flash flood warnings on November 19, and the St. Johns River near Cocoa reached **18.31 feet on November 19 and a reported 18.76 feet on November 20**, against a prior record of 17.55 feet set in 1953 ([Spectrum News 13](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2026/11/19/st-johns-river-in-cocoa-rises-above-record-level); [Yahoo/Florida Today](https://www.yahoo.com/news/weather-news/articles/live-updates-brevard-faces-more-100709458.html)). Hundreds of homes were reported still under water around Lake Poinsett, an NWS flood warning remained in effect on November 25, and AccuWeather's headline says river flooding could last into 2027 ([FOX 35](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/brevard-county-faces-worsening-flooding-residents-struggle-recover); [AccuWeather](https://www.accuweather.com/en/severe-weather/st-johns-river-smashes-73-year-record-in-florida-with-flooding-that-could-last-into-2027/1961124)).

The worst-hit places are south and west of Melbourne. In Palm Bay they include the Turkey Creek area (Sunswept Road), Badger Drive NE, Port Malabar Boulevard and Palm Bay Estates, a mobile-home community where about 65 homes were under mandatory evacuation November 19-21 after a bridge washout ([ClickOrlando](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/2026/11/17/live-updates/)). In West Melbourne they include Lamplighter Village, a 55-plus manufactured-home community evacuated by airboat, and the Hollywood Boulevard corridor ([Spectrum News 13](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2026/11/04/water-recedes-as-lamplighter-village-residents-assess-flood-damage)). The sheriff also named Barefoot Bay ([WFTV](https://www.wftv.com/news/local/thousands-report-damage-brevard-county-flood-recovery-continues/U4FFZDMKHRFXFLIIS4OYUROZ5Y/)). Inside Melbourne the reports are mostly street flooding: Jones Road, Evinrude Road, Eau Gallie's Pineapple Park and the Wickham Park area. Round two shifted the threat to Cocoa, Rockledge and Lake Poinsett.

Depth figures are single-home anecdotes, not surveyed high-water marks. Palm Bay homes near Turkey Creek reported **4 to 5 feet** ("up to our countertops"), another Palm Bay family reported 38 inches, one Lamplighter Village home had about 1.5 feet, and Melbourne street flooding near Wickham Park ran 8-10 inches ([Yahoo/Florida Today](https://www.yahoo.com/news/weather-news/articles/one-1-000-rain-event-100924007.html); [The Cool Down](https://www.thecooldown.com/outdoors/palm-bay-florida-flooding-rescues-homes/); [cfldr.com](https://www.cfldr.com/florida-flooding); [Yahoo/Florida Today](https://www.yahoo.com/news/weather-news/articles/flooding-florida-damage-homes-cars-224548308.html)). Two people died in Palm Bay in round one ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/2-dead-hundreds-rescued-amid-severe-flooding-florida/story?id=137819002)). The only event-level loss figures come second-hand through Wikipedia and conflict wildly: AccuWeather at $3-5 billion in total damage and economic loss, Aon at "exceeds $100 million" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2026_Florida_floods)). No Brevard residential damage total exists.

## Insurance pays up to $350,000, but only about one home in eight has it

Standard Florida homeowners and renters policies exclude rising-water damage, and local reporting on this flood confirms families are finding that out now ([WFTV](https://www.wftv.com/news/local/homeowners-insurance-may-not-help-with-central-florida-flooding/E6R6NI2OXVBELCYAQLPX5J3VQE/)). Only a separate flood policy pays. An NFIP policy covers up to **$250,000 on the building and $100,000 on contents**, with separate deductibles, and pays nothing for temporary housing or living expenses ([FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/how/what-is-covered)). It adds up to **$30,000 of Increased Cost of Compliance** money to elevate, floodproof or demolish when local floodplain rules require it ([CoveragePrep](https://coverageprep.com/guides/nfip-flood-insurance-claims/)). Sewer backup is covered only when flooding directly caused it. Flooded cars are covered only under the comprehensive part of an auto policy ([The Zebra](https://www.thezebra.com/auto-insurance/coverage/natural-disasters/does-car-insurance-cover-flood-damage/)).

Few Brevard owners hold that policy. Spectrum News 13 puts NFIP coverage at **11.8% of Brevard residential properties**; a commercial site says 17% of households ([Spectrum News 13](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2026/11/11/flood-coverage-is-the-largest-insurance-gap-experts-see); [fludzone.com](https://www.fludzone.com/flood-zones/florida/palm-bay)). Either way, 83-88% of homes have none. Take-up among the flooded homes was plausibly lower still, because inland Palm Bay is largely Zone X, where lenders do not require flood insurance, and this was rain and canal flooding rather than coastal surge; that is an inference, not a measured figure. Cotality reports **$18.2 billion** of Brevard residential value sits where flood insurance is not mandatory ([Cotality](https://www.cotality.com/hazard-hq/historic-rain-floods-floridas-space-coast)).

Insured owners face three dates. NFIP proof of loss is due **60 days after the loss**, which for a November 2 loss is about January 1, 2027 (my arithmetic); FEMA extended that window to 120 days after Hurricane Milton, but no extension or advance-payment bulletin for this flood was found ([United Policyholders](https://uphelp.org/claim-guidance-publications/flood-insurance-claim-deadlines/?print=print); [The Claim Organizer](https://theclaimorganizer.com/resources/nfip-flood-insurance-proof-of-loss)). The NFIP itself is authorized only through **December 11, 2026**, and new policies carry a 30-day wait, so a policy bought today could hit a lapse before it takes effect ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/flood-insurance/faq-national-flood-insurance-program-expires-december-11-2026); [Spectrum News 13](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/news/2026/11/19/homeowners-can-consider-national-and-private-flood-insurance-policies)). And every Citizens Property Insurance personal-lines policy except condo units must carry flood coverage from **January 1, 2027**, regardless of flood zone; the requirement already applied to dwellings insured at $400,000 or more ([Citizens](https://www.citizensfla.com/flood)).

For homeowners-policy and private-flood claims, Florida law requires the insurer to acknowledge within 7 days and pay or deny within 60, and bars claims noticed more than one year after the loss ([Fla. Stat. 627.70131](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/627.70131); [Fla. Stat. 627.70132](https://flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2025/627.70132)). Those rules and state mediation do not apply to NFIP claims. Assignment of benefits to contractors is prohibited on policies issued since January 1, 2023, so a contractor cannot take over the claim ([Florida Senate](https://www.flsenate.gov/Committees/BillSummaries/2022A/html/2)). One secondary source says public adjuster fees are capped at 10% for a year after a governor-declared emergency; the statute text was not seen ([DCS](https://www.dcspia.com/florida-insurance-claim-laws/)). As a payout benchmark, a third-party tabulation puts Florida's ten-year average paid NFIP claim at **$101,497** and says about one-third of Florida Helene claims closed without payment ([FloodHawk](https://floodhawk.com/florida/)). No claim counts or insured-loss figures for this flood have been published.

## FEMA caps grants at $46,400 and funds no permanent fix

The state asked for a declaration on November 9; the President approved **DR-4956 on Tuesday, November 24, 2026**, with an incident period beginning October 31 and continuing ([FOX 35](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/president-trump-approves-major-disaster-declaration-florida-flooding); [Space Coast Rocket](https://thespacecoastrocket.com/brevard-disaster-declaration-fema-4956-dr/)). A few outlets date the approval November 25. Individual Assistance covers six counties: **Brevard, Indian River, Martin, Okeechobee, Palm Beach and St. Lucie** ([Florida Phoenix](https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/11/25/disaster-declared-trump-approves-assistance-for-florida-floods/)). The state had asked for nine counties; DeSoto and Highlands appear to have been left out. A second expedited request for the November 17-20 round, covering Brevard, Broward, Flagler, Indian River, Orange, Putnam, Seminole and Volusia, was still pending on November 25 ([Yahoo/Florida Today](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/brevard-declared-disaster-area-eligible-141731252.html)). Because the DR-4956 incident period is open-ended, Brevard's round-two damage probably falls inside it already, but no source says so.

What FEMA pays an individual household is capped at **$46,400 in Housing Assistance and $46,400 in Other Needs Assistance**, the maximum for disasters declared on or after October 1, 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/06/2026-22693/notice-of-maximum-amount-of-assistance-under-the-individuals-and-households-program); [ClickOrlando](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/local/2026/11/25/heres-how-florida-storm-and-flood-victims-apply-for-fema-disaster-assistance/)). The same ClickOrlando report says most eligible victims receive a fraction of the cap. The money covers temporary housing, basic home repairs and uninsured personal property for owners and renters; FEMA pays only what insurance does not, and applicants must file with their insurer first. A one-time **$770 Serious Needs payment** exists on FEMA's fact sheet, with a 30-day application window from declaration; that amount may predate this year's adjustment ([FEMA](https://www.fema.gov/fact-sheet/serious-needs-assistance-0)). Owners in a mapped Special Flood Hazard Area who take FEMA money must then carry flood insurance for as long as they own the building ([FEMA](https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20250821/meeting-femas-federal-flood-insurance-requirement)).

Residents apply at DisasterAssistance.gov, on the FEMA app or at 800-621-3362, selecting "Severe Storms and Flooding (4956)." Filing the county's damage self-report does not register anyone with FEMA ([WFTV](https://www.wftv.com/news/local/brevard-residents-can-now-apply-fema-help-after-november-flooding/7B76K5UGV5GXLA7Z6EH4IFVZPA/)). **FEMA had posted no application deadline as of November 25.** The standard 60-day window would end about January 23, 2027, and the Serious Needs window about December 24, 2026; both dates are my arithmetic, not published deadlines. No Disaster Recovery Center had been announced for Brevard, and no registration counts or dollars approved exist yet ([Talk of Titusville](https://talkoftitusville.com/2026/11/25/fema-disaster-help-is-now-open-for-titusville-and-north-brevard/)).

On "how FEMA will fix this," the honest answer is that it is not doing so yet. Public Assistance for Brevard is limited to **debris removal and emergency protective measures** on a cost-share basis, which excludes permanent repair of roads, bridges, canals and drainage ([GSM Global News](https://gsmglobalnews.com/article/d20261125-sub6298-fldisaster1.html)). The Hazard Mitigation Grant Program, which pays for buyouts, elevations and drainage upgrades, is listed as **"under review"** and is not open ([Space Coast Rocket](https://thespacecoastrocket.com/brevard-disaster-declaration-fema-4956-dr/)). The cost-share percentage was not stated in any source, and no dollars have been obligated. A report of $400 million and $185.4 million in FEMA money for Florida on November 24 is not tied to this flood and likely concerns earlier disasters ([CiberCuba](https://en.cibercuba.com/noticias/2026-11-24-u1-e135253-s27061-nid346528-fema-anuncia-400-millones-dolares-florida-llegaran)).

The local problem is large and unfunded. Palm Bay's public works director told the council the Melbourne-Tillman canal system "filled, then flowed backward," that four canal crossings failed, and that road and bridge repair will cost **$10-15 million** ([The Palm Bayer](https://palmbayer.com/news/rcm-2026-11-05-recap/)). Brevard County was already $2.4 billion short on infrastructure before the flood ([FOX 35](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/funding-crisis-brevard-countys-2b-plus-shortfall-flooding-fix-infrastructure-projects)). No buyout program, stormwater bond or state resilience grant has been announced since.

Other federal help is thinner than the headlines suggest. Reports say DR-4956 includes SBA disaster loans, Disaster Unemployment Assistance and crisis counseling, but no SBA release, rates or deadlines for this event were found ([Talk of Titusville](https://talkoftitusville.com/2026/11/25/fema-disaster-help-is-now-open-for-titusville-and-north-brevard/); [Florida Phoenix](https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/11/25/disaster-declared-trump-approves-assistance-for-florida-floods/)). The best proxy is the October 2025 Brevard flood, where SBA offered homeowners up to $500,000 at rates as low as 3% for up to 30 years ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/article/2025/11/12/sba-offers-disaster-relief-florida-businesses-private-nonprofits-residents-affected-severe-storms-0)). No IRS relief, D-SNAP or HUD recovery money has been announced for this flood.

Brevard's 15-day wait was fast by current standards: declarations have averaged nearly 10 weeks under this administration, against three weeks or less historically ([WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/environment/2026-10-14/how-trumps-disaster-aid-delays-and-fema-cuts-could-galvanize-the-movable-middle-in-the-2026-elections)). The price of speed was a narrow package. The FEMA Review Council's May 2026 report recommends raising declaration thresholds and cutting the federal cost share to 50-75%, so the trend runs toward less federal money, not more ([NACo](https://www.naco.org/news/fema-review-council-releases-final-report-recommending-sweeping-changes)).

## State and local aid is small, and Palm Bay's names the trades

The state gives homeowners almost no direct cash. Its confirmed measures are the emergency orders (EO 26-227 on November 2, expanded November 4 and again about November 19), free muck-and-gut crews that have served 250-plus properties, and two resource centers in Palm Bay and West Melbourne that were listed as closing November 25 ([flgov.com](https://www.flgov.com/eog/news/press/2026/governor-ron-desantis-issues-updates-state-response-and-recovery-efforts-november)). For businesses, FloridaCommerce activated **$10 million in Emergency Bridge Loans**: interest-free, short-term, up to **$100,000** per small business, with Brevard among 21 eligible counties ([FDEM](https://www.floridadisaster.org/news-media/news/icymi-floridacommerce-activates-the-florida-small-business-emergency-bridge-loan-program-making-$10-million-available-for-businesses-impacted-by-the-2026-fall-flooding/)). A January 1, 2027 application deadline rests on a single local source. These are loans to flood-damaged businesses, so they matter to your clients only if their own shops were hit.

Two slower state benefits exist. Owners whose homes are uninhabitable for 30 days or more can claim a prorated property-tax refund by filing Form DR-465 with the property appraiser by **March 1, 2027** ([Florida DOR](https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/catastrophic_event_tax_refunds.pdf)). Elevate Florida, the state's elevation and buyout program, had approved only about 20 homeowner projects over six to eight weeks as of June 2026 and has not been reported open for this flood ([Bay News 9](https://baynews9.com/fl/tampa/news/2026/07/29/elevate-florida-applicants-still-waiting-for-funding-answers)). My Safe Florida Home covers wind, not flood.

Local help is more concrete. **Palm Bay and West Melbourne waived building-permit fees** for flood repairs; Palm Bay requires damage photos with the application and says flood-zone properties may not qualify, and West Melbourne's council adopted its waiver November 17 ([City of Palm Bay](https://www.palmbayfl.gov/government/city-departments-f-to-z/fire-rescue-and-emergency-management/november-flood-event); [West Melbourne](https://westmelbourne.gov/905/Emergency-Information)). No waiver was found for the City of Melbourne or unincorporated Brevard. Palm Bay also put about **$1 million of SHIP housing money** into a Disaster Relief Program for low- and moderate-income homeowners. Its eligible uses explicitly include **"emergency plumbing or electrical work,"** along with insurance deductibles, water extraction and removal of damaged materials ([City of Palm Bay](https://www.palmbayfl.gov/Home/Components/News/News/13796)). Owners must have held the homesteaded property for a year and be current on mortgage, taxes and utilities, and assistance above $20,000 carries a five-year forgivable lien. The pot is small: at $20,000 a home it covers about 50 houses. Brevard County adds a damage line (321-637-3818), 211 referrals, and a free volunteer cleanup portal; United Way and the Red Cross run volunteer muck-outs and case management ([Brevard Storm Center](https://www.brevardfl.gov/StormCenter)).

## Repair demand is rule-driven, and unlicensed work is a felony

Three rules push flooded owners to licensed trades regardless of budget. FPL will not reconnect a home with damaged customer-side equipment until a licensed electrician repairs it and FPL receives a passed inspection, and its fact sheet requires the electrician's license number to schedule the reconnect ([FPL](https://www.fpl.com/content/dam/fplgp/us/en/storm/pdf/Ready_to_safely_receive_power.pdf); [FPL](https://www.fpl.com/content/dam/fplgp/us/en/reliability/pdf/customer-repair-fact-sheet.pdf)). NEMA's water-damage guide marks submerged breakers, receptacles, switches, GFCI/AFCI devices and dry-location cable such as NM-B as **replace, not dry out** ([NEMA](https://www.nema.org/docs/default-source/standards-document-library/nema-gd-1-2019-evaluating-water-damaged-electrical-equipment-guide.pdf)). And the county is telling residents to confirm contractors pull permits ([ClickOrlando](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/local/2026/11/06/how-do-you-know-who-to-trust-palm-bay-flood-victims-warned-about-predatory-contractors/)). With reported depths of 1.5 to 5 feet, water reached well above outlet height in the worst homes, so a full below-waterline rewire is the likely scope there; that is my inference from the NEMA table, not a sourced scope. Sewage backing up into Palm Bay homes points to plumbing and sewer-lateral work as well ([FOX 35](https://www.fox35orlando.com/video/fmc-disfh6czd8cd3mca)).

Sizing the market honestly means admitting the data is thin. The only solid benchmark is FEMA's line that one inch of water causes roughly **$25,000** of damage ([NFIP](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/fema-nfip-just-one-inch-of-floodwater-flyer-06-2023.pdf)). Multiplying that by 1,000-2,000 homes gives $25-50 million of residential repair as a floor; this is illustrative arithmetic, and no source gives the electrical or plumbing share. No data was found on per-home trade costs, contractor backlogs or permit volumes in Brevard, so do not quote a trade-specific market size to clients.

Timing matters more than total size. South Brevard homes dried out within days and owners were tearing out floors and walls by November 9 ([Spectrum News 13](https://mynews13.com/fl/orlando/weather/2026/11/09/a-week-later-brevard-residents-slowly-recovering-from-devastating-flooding)). Those gutted homes need electrical and plumbing rough-in and inspection before drywall, but most owners are uninsured and FEMA money only opened this week. Expect funded jobs to build from December 2026 through the first quarter of 2027 as FEMA grants and NFIP claims pay. Homes along the St. Johns near Cocoa, Rockledge and Lake Poinsett are a second, later wave, since many are still under water.

The contractor rules are strict and are also the marketing message. Under Florida Statute 489.127, unlicensed contracting during a governor-declared state of emergency is a **third-degree felony**, and that emergency has been in force in Brevard since November 2 ([Fla. Stat. 489.127](https://m.flsenate.gov/statutes/489.127)). County emergency management and local TV warned residents on November 6 and again on November 24 about unsolicited offers, high-pressure sales and large upfront payments, and told them to get scope, total price and completion date in writing ([WFTV](https://www.wftv.com/news/local/homeowners-warned-avoid-unlicensed-contractors-after-recent-flooding/4POEGFAYCRDZRK62IIVXOLNBSQ/)). No price-gouging hotline notice or DBPR emergency order for this event was found, and Florida's price-gouging, solicitation and cancellation statutes were not checked in this research; have a lawyer or the statute text confirm those before running claims-related or urgency-based advertising.

## Conclusion

The opportunity is real but narrower and slower than a headline flood suggests. Homeowners here are mostly uninsured, capped at $46,400 of FEMA help that few will receive in full, and already being coached to distrust anyone who knocks on the door. The contractors who win are local, licensed firms that lead with the license number, handle the permit (fee waived in Palm Bay and West Melbourne), know the FPL reconnect process, give written fixed-scope estimates, and can work inside the payment paths owners actually have: FEMA grants, NFIP claims and Palm Bay's SHIP program. Because no drainage fix is funded and the same neighborhoods flooded twice in three weeks, resilience work has a longer tail than the one-time repair spike.

Four things will change the picture and are worth checking weekly: the FEMA application deadline and any Brevard recovery center on fema.gov/disaster/4956; whether the second declaration request is approved and whether Public Assistance expands to permanent work; whether Congress extends the NFIP past December 11; and whether an SBA loan declaration with real terms appears. Until the first FEMA registration and award totals are published, the true number of flooded houses stays unknown, and any campaign should be sized to roughly 1,000-2,000 Brevard homes rather than "thousands."
